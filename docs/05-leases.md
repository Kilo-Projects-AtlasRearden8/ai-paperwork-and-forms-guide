# 5. Leases and rental agreements

A lease is a legal contract. AI can help you read it, spot important clauses, and come up with questions — but AI cannot give legal advice or negotiate for you.

## Reading a lease with AI

Before you sign, upload the lease (without secrets — see Chapter 7) and ask:

- "What is the monthly rent, and when is it due?"
- "How long is the lease, and what happens when it ends?"
- "How much is the security deposit, and when is it returned?"
- "Who is responsible for repairs and maintenance?"
- "Are there late fees, pet fees, or parking fees?"
- "Can I sublet or have roommates?"
- "How much notice do I need to give to move out?"

## Clauses to watch for

Ask AI to point out these common lease terms:

| Clause | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Rent amount and due date | Know exactly what you owe and when |
| Late fees | Penalties for paying after the due date |
| Security deposit | How much you pay upfront and how it's returned |
| Lease term | Month-to-month, 6 months, 1 year, etc. |
| Renewal terms | Whether the lease automatically renews |
| Notice to vacate | How much warning you must give before moving out |
| Repairs and maintenance | Who fixes what, and how to request repairs |
| Subletting and guests | Rules for roommates, Airbnb, or long-term guests |
| Early termination | Fees or penalties for breaking the lease early |

## Questions to ask your landlord

AI can help you draft questions, such as:

- "Is the rent amount final, or are there additional monthly fees?"
- "Who do I contact for emergency repairs, and what is the response time?"
- "Can I have a pet, and is there a pet deposit or monthly pet fee?"
- "What happens if I need to break the lease early?"
- "Is parking included, or is there a separate fee?"

## What NOT to do with AI and leases

- Do not let AI rewrite your lease or draft legal clauses for you to sign.
- Do not share your Social Security number, driver's license, or bank details in an AI chat.
- Do not rely on AI to tell you whether a lease is "fair" or "enforceable." Laws vary by state and city.

## Example: reviewing a lease section

You paste a lease clause (with names and addresses removed) and ask:

> "This section says the landlord can enter the unit with 24 hours notice. Is that normal?"

AI might explain that entry notice rules are common but vary by state, and suggest you check your state's landlord-tenant laws or ask a tenants' rights organization.

## Reminder

This guide is educational only. It is not legal advice. Leases are legally binding contracts. Before you sign, consider having a lawyer, legal aid office, or tenants' rights group review the lease.
