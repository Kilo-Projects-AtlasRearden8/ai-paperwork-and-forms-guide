2. Reading forms with AI

Forms can be long, confusing, and full of fine print. AI can help you understand what a form is asking before you fill anything out.

How to read a form with AI

Step 1: Get a copy of the form

  • Save a PDF, photo, or scan of the form to your computer.
  • If the form is on paper, take a clear photo of each page.

Step 2: Share it with AI

  • Upload the file to your AI tool, or paste the text if you can copy it.
  • Only upload forms that do not contain secrets (see Chapter 7). Remove or black out sensitive details first if needed.

Step 3: Ask for a plain-English summary

Try prompts like these:

  • "Can you summarize this form in plain English? What is it for?"
  • "What are the most important sections I need to pay attention to?"
  • "What dates, deadlines, or fees are mentioned?"
  • "Do I need to sign anything? Where?"
  • "What documents will I need to attach?"

Step 4: Check against the original

AI can misread handwriting, miss checkboxes, or misunderstand fine print. Always open the original form and verify:

  • Names and addresses are correct
  • Dates and deadlines match
  • Checkboxes and signatures are in the right places

Example: reading a rental application

You upload a rental application and ask:

"This is a rental application. What information is it asking for, and what should I watch out for?"

AI might reply:

  • It asks for your current address, employer, income, and references.
  • There is an application fee and a credit check authorization.
  • Watch out: the application authorizes a background check.

You then read the original to confirm the fee amount and the background-check wording.

If the form is hard to read

  • Ask AI to break the form into sections and explain one section at a time.
  • Ask for a checklist: "Make me a checklist of everything I need to complete this form."
  • Ask AI to define words you don't know: "What does 'joint and several liability' mean in plain English?"

What to watch for in any form

Look for these common items:

  • Deadlines — dates by which something must be returned or signed
  • Fees — application fees, processing fees, penalties
  • Signatures — where you or others must sign or initial
  • Attachments — documents you must include (pay stubs, ID, references)
  • Authorization language — permission to run credit checks, contact employers, or share your data
