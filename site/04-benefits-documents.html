4. Benefits documents

Benefits paperwork — unemployment, disability, food assistance, housing aid, and similar programs — can feel overwhelming. AI can help you read letters, understand requirements, and draft responses. It cannot file claims for you or guarantee approval.

Reading a benefits letter

Benefits letters often contain decisions, deadlines, and appeal rights. Ask AI:

  • "What decision does this letter make?"
  • "What is the deadline to respond?"
  • "What happens if I miss the deadline?"
  • "What documents do I need to provide?"
  • "How do I appeal this decision if I disagree?"

Then check the original letter yourself. Deadlines and appeal instructions are usually in bold or near the end of the letter.

Filling out a benefits application

Applications often ask for income, household size, expenses, and medical information. AI can help you:

  • Organize your answers before you enter them online
  • Draft explanations for gaps in employment or income
  • Make a checklist of documents to gather (pay stubs, bank statements, letters from doctors)

Do not enter your full Social Security number or banking details into an AI chat. Keep those for the official application site only.

Drafting an appeal or request

If a benefit is denied or reduced, you may be able to appeal. AI can help you draft a request, such as:

  • "I am writing to appeal the denial of benefits dated [date]. I disagree because..."
  • "Please reconsider my application. I have enclosed [document]..."

Keep your appeal short, factual, and polite. Include dates, case numbers, and document references from the original letter.

Example: understanding a denial letter

You upload a denial letter and ask:

"This letter says my application was denied. What is the reason, and what is the deadline to appeal?"

AI might respond:

  • Reason: income appears above the limit based on the pay stub provided.
  • Deadline: appeals must be filed within 30 days of the letter date.

You open the letter, confirm the 30-day deadline and the appeal address, then decide whether to appeal with new documents.

Common benefits programs

  • Unemployment insurance
  • Disability benefits (short-term or long-term)
  • Food assistance (SNAP)
  • Housing assistance and rental aid
  • Medical assistance and prescription aid

Program names and rules vary by state. AI can explain general concepts but cannot tell you your state's specific rules.

Safety reminders

  • Never paste your Social Security number, case number, or banking details into an AI chat.
  • Benefits decisions can affect your income and housing. Have a benefits counselor, caseworker, or legal aid office review important letters.

Reminder. This guide is educational only. It is not legal or financial advice. Benefits rules vary by state and change over time. Contact your local benefits office or a legal aid organization for help with your specific case.
